About Me

How I Found Data Science

I’ve always been a pretty analytical person. One of my mom’s favorite childhood memories is how I would constantly ask her to go through math flashcards with me. As I got older, that interest in problem solving naturally led me toward computer science. With two brothers who studied computer science, I initially decided to follow a similar path and started college studying the same field.

That changed during a conversation with a family friend I met while playing pickleball. He worked in data science, and after talking with him about his work, I became interested in the combination of mathematics, programming, and problem solving that the field offered. I ended up shadowing him and got a better look at how data could be used to approach real-world problems from different angles.

That combination clicked with me. I realized I didn’t have to choose between the parts of programming and mathematics that I enjoyed—I could bring them together to solve problems, find patterns, and develop data-driven solutions.

That led me to switch my focus to Statistics with an emphasis in Data Science at BYU. Since then, I’ve explored data science through coursework, projects, research, and professional experience, and I’ve become especially interested in using data and technology to solve practical problems.

My Journey

📚 2021 — Started at BYU

I began studying Statistics at Brigham Young University in September 2021.

💻 2022 — Junior Programmer, PAIC Solar

My first professional programming experience was at PAIC Solar, where I worked on automating business processes and improving internal tools.

This experience helped me see how programming and technology could be used to solve problems that businesses deal with every day.

🌎 2022–2024 — Volunteer Representative

From March 2022 to March 2024, I served as a full-time representative in Newport Beach, California.

During this time, I led teams, organized events, and worked with people from a wide variety of backgrounds.

Although this wasn’t a technical role, it taught me skills that I now value just as much as technical knowledge: communication, leadership, organization, and working with people to accomplish a goal.

📚 2024 — Returned to BYU

I returned to BYU in April 2024 to continue my Statistics degree, this time with an emphasis in Data Science.

Since returning, I’ve focused on statistical analysis, machine learning, programming, and data science through coursework, research, and projects.

👨‍🏫 2025–2026 — Teaching Assistant

I worked as a Teaching Assistant for introductory statistics at BYU, helping students understand statistical concepts, work through coursework, and develop their data analysis skills.

Working with students taught me how to take technical concepts and explain them in a way that makes sense to people with different levels of experience. It also strengthened my communication and problem-solving skills—both of which I find valuable when working with data and collaborating with others.

🔬 2026 — Research Assistant

In January 2026, I began working as a research assistant at BYU studying machine learning and information theory.

My research involves building and training neural networks in PyTorch and exploring concepts such as PCA, entropy, and information theory.

This experience has given me a deeper interest in understanding not just whether a machine learning model works, but why it works and what it has learned.

🎓 2026 — B.S. Statistics

In August 2026, I completed my B.S. in Statistics with an emphasis in Data Science.

After returning to BYU in April 2024, I completed the remainder of my degree in approximately 2.5 years.

🎓 Fall 2026 — M.S. Statistics

In Fall 2026, I’ll begin my Master’s in Statistics at BYU.

I’m looking forward to continuing to develop my statistical and machine learning skills while exploring problems that connect theory with practical applications.

What I’m Interested In

I’m particularly interested in the intersection of data science, machine learning, and business.

I don’t want to use machine learning simply because a problem can be solved with machine learning. I’m more interested in starting with a real problem and figuring out whether data, statistics, automation, or AI can actually make a meaningful difference.

Some areas I’m especially interested in include:

  • Using data to improve business decisions
  • Understanding customer behavior
  • Finding inefficiencies in business processes
  • Predicting outcomes and identifying trends
  • Automating repetitive work
  • Building AI systems that solve practical problems
  • Using statistics and machine learning to turn data into actionable insights

What I’m Doing Now

Right now, I’m focused on three things: graduate study, machine learning research, and building practical data-driven projects.

I’m beginning my M.S. in Statistics at BYU, where I’ll continue developing my foundation in statistical modeling, machine learning, and data science.

I’m also continuing my research in machine learning and information theory. My current work involves studying how neural networks learn and represent information, using concepts from information theory and tools such as PyTorch and PCA. I’m particularly interested in understanding what happens inside a model rather than treating it as a black box.

Outside of school and research, I’m building projects that explore how data and AI can solve practical problems. One project I’m currently developing investigates whether AI agents can automate employee scheduling for businesses, taking into account employee availability, preferences, and business constraints.

These experiences are helping me explore the kind of work I want to pursue long-term: using statistics, machine learning, and technology to understand problems and build solutions that have a practical impact.

Outside of Data Science

When I’m not working with data, I’m usually doing something active or competitive. I enjoy volleyball, pickleball, rock climbing, and pretty much any game where I can compete with friends. I’m also a big fan of fantasy novels and survival video games.
